Title:
Why Plane Windows Have a Tiny Hole

Description:
That tiny hole near the bottom of a plane window is there on purpose: it lets cabin air into the gap between the panes, so the outer pane carries the pressure and the middle pane stays ready as a spare. This short animated explainer walks through the three panes, the pressure difference at cruising height, and the hole's second job of letting moisture out.

What is covered:
- Three panes: outer, middle (with the hole) and an inner scratch pane that carries no load
- At about 35,000 ft, outside air presses about a third as hard as cabin air (about 3.4 vs 11 psi)
- The cabin feels like a mountain about 7,000 ft high
- How the breather hole balances the middle pane and puts the load on the outer pane
- Quick question: why load just one pane?
- The middle pane as a spare: rated for 1.5 times normal pressure in a Boeing 737 manual
- The second job: letting moisture out so the window fogs and frosts less

The window and cross-sections are simplified drawings. "Half a kilo on every fingertip-sized patch" is our own arithmetic from the pressures above (11 - 3.4 psi is about 0.53 kg per square centimetre).
