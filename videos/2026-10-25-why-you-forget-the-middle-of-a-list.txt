Title:
Your Memory Has a Weak Spot: The Middle of a List

Description:
Hear a list of words and you will usually remember the first few and the last few, but lose the middle. This short animated explainer shows why, using classic memory experiments, and how to use it when you study or speak.

What is covered:
- The serial position effect: the U-shaped memory curve
- Murdock's 1962 test: lists of 10 to 40 words, a U shape at every length
- Why the start sticks: early words get the most rehearsal (Rundus, 1971)
- Why the end sticks: the last words are still fresh in short-term memory
- Quick question: what 30 seconds of counting does to the end of the list (Glanzer and Cunitz, 1966)
- First impressions: Asch's 1946 six-trait experiment
- Three ways to use it: put what matters first or last, review the middle first, write long lists down

The bar charts show the typical shape and are illustrative, not exact data.
