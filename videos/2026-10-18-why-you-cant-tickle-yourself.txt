Title: Why You Can't Tickle Yourself
Audience: No, it's not made for kids. Category: Education. Visibility: Public.
Tags: why can't you tickle yourself, tickling science, brain prediction, efference copy, science explained
Description:
You can't tickle yourself, but a robot can make your own movement tickle you if the touch arrives a fifth of a second late. This short animated explainer shows how your brain predicts your own touch and turns it down.

What is covered:
- Why your own fingers on your palm feel like almost nothing
- How the brain keeps a copy of every movement command and predicts the feeling (an efference copy)
- 1998 brain scans: touch areas are less active when you touch yourself, and the cerebellum's role
- Quick question: why would the brain turn down your own touch?
- The 1999 robot test: a 0.2-second delay, or a twisted direction, brings the tickle back
- The same trick keeps the room still when your eyes move
- A 2003 finger-pressing study: why each person pushes back about 40% harder

The tickle meters and the force bars are simplified for teaching.
