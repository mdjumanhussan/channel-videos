Title:
Banks Don't Lend Out Your Savings

Description:
When a bank makes a loan, it doesn't hand over other people's savings: it creates a new deposit in the borrower's account, and repaying the loan makes that money disappear. This short animated explainer follows one illustrative car loan, using the Bank of England's and the Reserve Bank of Australia's own explanations.

What is covered:
- The story many of us learned: the bank as a middleman
- One car loan: two new lines in the bank's books
- Most money is bank deposits: in Australia in 2018, about $75 billion of notes and coins out of about $2 trillion
- Quick question: when you repay a loan, where does the money go?
- Why repaying a loan (or a credit card) destroys money
- Three limits: profitable lending, capital rules, interest rates
- Why deposits still matter to your bank

The car loan, balances and credit card are illustrative examples. This video explains how banking works; it is not financial advice.
