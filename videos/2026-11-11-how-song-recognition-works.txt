Title: How Your Phone Names a Song From a Noisy Room

Description:
Your phone can name a song in a noisy café from a few seconds of sound. This short animated explainer shows the audio fingerprinting method that Shazam's Avery Wang described in 2003: a picture of the sound, a map of its loudest points, pairs of points turned into codes, and a match found by lining those codes up in time.

What is covered:
- Turning sound into a picture (a spectrogram)
- Keeping only the loudest points: the "constellation map"
- Why chatter and clinking cups don't stop it
- Quick question: why isn't one loud note enough?
- Pairs of points plus their time gap: codes about a million times more specific
- Lining up the codes: the tallest pile wins
- The limits: live recordings

The spectrogram, star maps, example code and pile heights are illustrative drawings.
