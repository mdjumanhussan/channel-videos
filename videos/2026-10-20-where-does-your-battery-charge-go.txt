Title: Your Battery Is Sealed. So Where Does Its Charge Go?
Audience: No, it's not made for kids. Category: Education. Visibility: Public.
Tags: phone battery, battery health, lithium ion battery, why batteries wear out, science explained
Description:
Your phone battery is sealed and nothing leaks out, yet every year a full charge lasts a little less. This short animated explainer shows where the lost capacity goes: lithium gets trapped in a thin crust inside the battery.

What is covered:
- How lithium ions shuttle between the metal oxide and the graphite when you charge and use your phone
- The thin crust (the solid electrolyte interphase, or SEI) that forms on the graphite
- Why lithium trapped in that crust can no longer carry charge
- What one charge cycle is, and Apple's figures: about 80% capacity after 500 cycles (older iPhones) or 1,000 (iPhone 15 models)
- Quick question: a full phone in a hot car, or a 40% phone in a cool room?
- Battery University's estimates for one year of storage at different temperatures and charge levels
- What helps: avoid heat, avoid long hours at 100%, and small top-ups are fine

The battery drawings are simplified for teaching.
