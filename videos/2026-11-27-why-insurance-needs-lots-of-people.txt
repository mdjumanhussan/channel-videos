Title:
Why Insurance Only Works With Lots of People

Description:
Insurance works by pooling: many people each pay a little, and that money covers the few who have a loss that year. This short animated explainer shows why the trick only works with a big group, and what can break it.

What is covered:
- Your house burns down: who really pays to rebuild it?
- Risk pooling: premiums from everyone, claims for a few
- A textbook example: 100,000 people, about 250 claims of $1,000, so $2.50 each
- Why 10 cars are a gamble: about one year in four, two or more are stolen
- Quick question: how predictable are 100,000 cars?
- The law of large numbers, with coin flips
- What a premium pays for: expected claims, running costs, profit and a cushion
- Why one hurricane hitting one stretch of coast could empty the pool, and why insurers spread their risk

The $300,000 house and $1,000 premium are illustrative. The theft bars are simulated (a 1 in 10 chance per car); the coin flips and premium sizes are examples. 'Within a few hundred' is our own arithmetic for 100,000 cars at 1 in 10. This video explains how insurance works; it is not financial advice.
