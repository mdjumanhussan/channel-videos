Title:
The Internet Isn't in the Sky. It's Under the Sea.

Description:
About 99% of the data that crosses between countries travels through cables on the ocean floor, not through satellites. This short animated explainer shows how thin those cables are, how laser light carries your data inside them, how boosters get it across an ocean, and what really breaks them.

What is covered:
- The route of a video call between countries: down to the sea floor, not up to space
- More than 500 cables, about 1.7 million km in all (over 40 times around Earth)
- Deep-sea cables are roughly as thick as a garden hose; near the shore they get steel armour
- Hair-thin glass fibres carry data as flashes of laser light
- Quick question: how does light cross a whole ocean?
- Boosters along the cable, powered through a copper layer
- 150 to 200 cable incidents a year: about 80% from ship anchors and fishing, under 1% from sharks and other fish (up to 2006)
- Repairs take days to months, and what happened to Tonga in 2022

The globe, cable drawings and the cables-vs-satellites bars are simplified and illustrative. "Over 40 times around Earth" is our own arithmetic (1.7 million km / about 40,000 km).
