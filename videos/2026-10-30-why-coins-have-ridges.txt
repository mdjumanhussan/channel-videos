Title:
The Real Reason Coins Have Ridges

Description:
Run your thumb around the edge of many coins and you feel tiny ridges. They started as a security system against clipping, when people shaved silver off coins, and this short animated explainer shows how they worked and why they are still there.

What is covered:
- When coins were made of silver and gold, and worth the metal inside them
- Clipping: shaving thin slivers off hand-struck coins and selling the silver
- Why clipped money kept shrinking as it was passed on
- The fix: machine-pressed coins with a ridged (milled) edge, so a shaved coin is easy to spot
- Quick question: which coin should the shopkeeper refuse?
- England's machine-made coins from 1662, the 1696 recoinage, and Isaac Newton at the Royal Mint
- Why ridges stayed: tradition, harder to fake, and telling coins apart by touch (119 ridges on a US quarter, 118 on a dime)

The coin drawings are simplified for teaching.
