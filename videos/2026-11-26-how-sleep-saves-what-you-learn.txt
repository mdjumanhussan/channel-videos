Title:
Sleep Is When Learning Sticks

Description:
People who sleep after learning something forget less than people who stay awake for the same time. This short animated explainer shows what the brain seems to be doing at night: replaying the day in deep sleep and, scientists think, moving new memories from the hippocampus into the cortex.

What is covered:
- Same list, two nights: why the sleeper remembers more
- 1924: less forgetting after sleep than after being awake
- One early idea: nothing new crowds in while you sleep
- The hippocampus (a quick notepad) and the cortex (the long-term library)
- Replay: in rats, the same brain cells fire again during sleep, in the same order, up to 20 times faster
- Quick question: REM sleep or deep sleep?
- Slow brain waves, sleep spindles, and the leading theory of how memories move
- A UC Berkeley study: people who napped 100 minutes learned new names and faces about 20% better
- Why timing matters, and what an all-nighter skips

The word lists, syllable cards, brain, track and bars are simplified drawings; the bars show the reported 20% difference, not raw scores. This video explains how memory works; it is not medical advice.
