Title:
3 Seconds From Flash to Thunder = About 1 km

Description:
Count the seconds between a lightning flash and its thunder, divide by three, and you get roughly how many kilometres away it struck. This short animated explainer shows what thunder actually is, why the flash beats the sound, and why thunder sometimes cracks and sometimes rumbles.

What is covered:
- The count: one, two, three, boom
- What thunder is: air heated to about 30,000 °C in a few millionths of a second
- Why the squeezed air bursts outward, and how the shock wave becomes ordinary sound
- Light (about 300,000 km a second) vs sound (about a third of a kilometre a second)
- Quick question: nine seconds, how far?
- The rule: seconds ÷ 3 = km (÷ 5 for miles)
- Why thunder cracks when close and rumbles when far
- How far thunder carries (usually about 15 km, up to about 25 km on a still night)
- The rule to keep: when thunder roars, go indoors, and wait 30 minutes after the last rumble

The drawings are simplified and not to scale. "Close to a million times faster" is our own arithmetic (about 300,000 km/s ÷ about 0.34 km/s). The count gives a rough distance only; it is not a safety test.
