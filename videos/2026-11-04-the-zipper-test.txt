LONG VIDEO (post 18:00 Sydney)
Title: The Zipper Test: You Know Less Than You Feel

Description:
Most people feel they understand everyday things, until they try to explain them. This short animated explainer shows the Yale test behind the illusion of explanatory depth, why a bike chain trips up so many people, and the simple fix when you study.

What is covered:
- The bike chain mistake made by over a third of non-cyclists in one study
- The 2002 Yale test: rate your understanding, then explain it step by step
- How a zipper really closes: the Y-shaped channel in the slider
- Quick question: does a warning stop the illusion?
- Why capitals, recipes and movie plots are mostly spared
- 44% vs 12%: what happened with a real bike in front of people
- A 2023 study: explaining a zipper lowered confidence about snow
- The fix: explain it out loud, and study where you get stuck

Numbers marked illustrative and the drawings are simplified for teaching. The 3.9, 3.1, 2.5, 44% and 12% figures come from the studies below.
