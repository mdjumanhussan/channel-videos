Title:
A Year Isn't 365 Days

Description:
A year is not 365 days: Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to come back to the same season, so the calendar needs a leap day. This short animated explainer shows how fast the calendar would drift without it, why 1900 was not a leap year, and how close today's calendar is to Earth's real year.

What is covered:
- The real year: about 365.2422 days, almost six hours more than 365
- The drift: almost a day every four years, about 24 days a century
- The fix: a leap day every fourth year (Julian calendar, 46 BC)
- Quick question: was 1900 a leap year?
- Why a quarter day is slightly too much, and the 10 days skipped in 1582
- The century rule: 1700, 1800 and 1900 had no leap day; 2000 did; 2100 won't
- How close it is now: one day out in more than 3,000 years

The 750-year season swap is our own arithmetic from the figures above; the orbit and calendar pages are simplified drawings.
