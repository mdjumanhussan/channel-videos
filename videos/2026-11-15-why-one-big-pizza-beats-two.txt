Title:
Why One 18-Inch Pizza Beats Two 12-Inch Pizzas

Description:
One 18-inch pizza gives you more pizza than two 12-inch pizzas, even though two sounds like more. This short animated explainer shows the simple square rule behind it, how to compare pizza prices fairly, and the one case where two pizzas win.

What is covered:
- Why pizza size is the width, but you eat the area
- The square rule: twice as wide means four times the area
- The numbers: 113, 226 and 254 square inches
- Quick question: is a 16-inch pizza twice an 8-inch one?
- What NPR found in 74,476 pizza prices (2014)
- Price per square inch, and why you should check the inches, not the size names
- The twist: two pizzas have about a third more crust
- The same rule for TV screens and cake tins

The prices per square inch shown on screen and the bar heights are illustrative. Areas are rounded.
