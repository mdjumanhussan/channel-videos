Title: Your Goosebumps Are Fluffing Fur You Don't Have
Category: Education

Description:
Cold, fear or a song you love can raise tiny bumps on your skin. This short animated explainer shows the muscle and nerve behind goosebumps, what raised fur does for animals, and what a 2020 study in mice found the same nerve also does.

What is covered:
- The arrector pili: the tiny muscle that pulls each hair upright
- The fight-or-flight nerve that flips the switch
- Why raised fur keeps a snow monkey warm
- Why a scared cat puffs up
- Why it barely helps humans
- Quick question: what else does the goosebump nerve do in mice?
- The 2020 Cell study: the same nerve helps grow new hair (in mice)
- Chills from music, and why they often come without bumps

The drawings are simplified for teaching and are not to scale. The mouse findings have not yet been shown in people.
