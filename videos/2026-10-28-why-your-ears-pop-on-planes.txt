Title:
Your Ears Have a Tiny Door. Here's Why They Pop

Description:
Your ears have a tiny tube that opens when you swallow, and on a plane it decides whether your ears feel fine or blocked and sore. This short animated explainer shows why your ears pop on takeoff and landing, and why landing is often the harder part.

What is covered:
- Air pressure: why there is less push up a mountain, and what cabin pressure is like
- The snack bag that puffs up on a flight
- Inside your ear: the middle ear, the eardrum and the Eustachian tube
- Going up: the eardrum bulges out, and extra air slips out by itself
- Quick question: which way does your eardrum bend when you land?
- Coming down: why you have to open the tube by swallowing or yawning
- What helps: swallow, yawn, chew gum, stay awake for landing; why young children and colds have more trouble

The ear diagram is simplified for teaching. This is general information, not medical advice. If ear pain lasts more than a few days, see a doctor.
