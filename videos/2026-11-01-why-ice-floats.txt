=== LONG VIDEO ===
Title:
Ice Breaks a Rule Almost Every Solid Follows

Description:
For most substances, the solid sinks in its own liquid, yet the ice in your drink floats. This short animated explainer shows how water's hydrogen bonds lock ice into open, six-sided rings, and why that matters for icebergs, burst pipes and fish in frozen lakes.

What is covered:
- What decides whether something floats: how tightly it is packed (the cork example)
- Why the solid form of most substances is packed tighter than the liquid, so it sinks
- The water molecule: one oxygen, two hydrogens, and the weak pulls called hydrogen bonds
- Freezing: the bonds lock molecules into a honeycomb with open space, so ice takes about 9% more room
- Quick question: how much of an iceberg is under the water?
- Why water pipes can burst in a hard frost
- Why lakes freeze from the top, with water densest at about 4 °C, so fish survive under the ice

The molecule, lattice and lake drawings are simplified for teaching.
