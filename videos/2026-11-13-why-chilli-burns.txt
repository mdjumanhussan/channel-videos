Title: There's No Heat in Chilli. So What Burns?

Description:
A chilli straight from the fridge is cold, yet it still burns. This short animated explainer shows the heat sensor in your nerves that chilli tricks, the Nobel-winning discovery behind it, and why milk calms the burn better than water.

What is covered:
- TRPV1: a heat gate on your nerve endings that opens near 43 °C
- Capsaicin: the chilli molecule that opens the gate with no heat at all
- How the gate was found in 1997 (2021 Nobel Prize)
- The cold version: menthol, mint and TRPM8
- Quick question: water or milk?
- Why water spreads capsaicin and milk's casein carries it away (a 2019 study of seven drinks)
- Why birds can eat chillies without feeling the burn

Gates, molecules, cells and drink icons are simplified drawings for teaching.
