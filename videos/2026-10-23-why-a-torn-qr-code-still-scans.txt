Title: Tear a Corner Off a QR Code. It Still Scans. Here's Why
Audience: No, it's not made for kids. Category: Science & Technology. Visibility: Public.
Tags: how qr codes work, qr code, qr code error correction, reed solomon, technology explained
Description:
Tear a corner off a QR code or spill coffee on it, and your phone can usually still read it. This short animated explainer shows how: every QR code carries spare bytes that let your phone rebuild the missing parts.

What is covered:
- Why QR codes were invented (Denso Wave, 1994), when a barcode held only about 20 characters
- The three big corner squares and their rare 1:1:3:1:1 pattern
- How the small squares store your message as bytes
- Reed-Solomon error correction: spare bytes that rebuild lost ones
- The four levels: about 7%, 15%, 25% or 30% of the data can be rebuilt
- Quick question: which corner is safest to lose?
- Why a small logo in the middle still scans, and a big one doesn't

The QR drawings are simplified for teaching and are not real codes.
