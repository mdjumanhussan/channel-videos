Title: Why a Wrong Guess Helps You Learn

Description:
Guessing an answer before you are taught it, even when the guess is wrong, can help you remember the right answer better than just studying. This short animated explainer walks through the 2009 experiments behind this "pretesting effect", the two conditions it needs, and a simple way to use it when you study.

What is covered:
- A guess you can try yourself: whale and ...?
- The 2009 word-pair experiment: 13 seconds of study vs 8 seconds of guessing plus 5 seconds with the answer
- Why the guessers were almost always wrong (right only about 5% of the time)
- Quick question: who remembered more?
- The result: about 10% better recall, right away and about a day and a half later
- It works with real reading too: questions before an essay about vision
- Why it might work, and what scientists still debate
- Two rules: see the answer soon after, and the guess needs a link to the answer
- How to use it: turn headings into questions and guess before you read

The word cards, bars and grids are simplified drawings; the bar heights and the 1-in-20 grid are illustrative.
