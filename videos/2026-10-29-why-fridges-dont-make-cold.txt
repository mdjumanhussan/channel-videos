Title:
Fridges Don't Make Cold

Description:
The air behind your fridge is warm, and that warmth came out of your food. This short animated explainer shows how a fridge moves heat out of a cold box and into your kitchen, using two simple tricks.

What is covered:
- What cold really is: heat taken away, and why heat only flows from warm to cooler by itself
- The refrigerant: a fluid that carries heat round a loop of pipes, like a courier
- Trick one: evaporating takes heat (why you feel cold after a shower); isobutane boils at about -12 °C
- Trick two: squeezing a gas heats it (the warm bike pump), and the compressor
- The coils on the back, and the narrow tube that makes the refrigerant very cold
- Quick question: does leaving the fridge door open cool your kitchen?
- Why the back is warm, how an air conditioner does the same thing, and why thick frost makes a fridge work less well

The fridge and pipe diagrams are simplified for teaching, and the heat bar is not to scale.
