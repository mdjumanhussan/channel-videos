Title:
That Stomach Growl? Your Gut Is Cleaning House

Description:
That growl from your belly is muscle squeezing gas and liquid through your stomach and small intestine, and it is loudest when there is no food inside to muffle it. This short animated explainer also shows the cleaning wave your gut sends through about every one to two hours between meals, and why eating switches it off.

What is covered:
- The name: borborygmi, a Greek word that imitates the rumble
- Where it comes from: the stomach and, just as often, the small intestine
- Rings of muscle squeezing food, liquid and gas along
- Why an empty gut is louder: more air than food, nothing to muffle the sound
- Quick question: how often does the cleaning wave come?
- The migrating motor complex: a strong wave every one to two hours that clears leftover food, mucus and bacteria
- Its cycle: a quiet spell of about 45 to 60 minutes, a build-up, then a burst of strong waves for 5 to 15 minutes
- Motilin, and why eating switches the wave off

The gut drawings and timeline are simplified. This video explains how digestion works; it is not medical advice.
