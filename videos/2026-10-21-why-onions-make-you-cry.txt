Title: The Onion's Chemical Trap: Why Cutting It Makes You Cry
Audience: No, it's not made for kids. Category: Education. Visibility: Public.
Tags: why do onions make you cry, onion tears, cutting onions, kitchen science, science explained
Description:
A whole onion never makes you cry, but cut it and your eyes sting within about 30 seconds. This short animated explainer shows the chemical trap inside every onion cell, and how to cry less.

What is covered:
- The glow-stick idea: two ingredients kept apart until something breaks them
- Inside an onion cell: sulfur compounds, and an enzyme (alliinase) sealed in a pocket
- How two enzymes turn them into a stinging gas, syn-propanethial-S-oxide, which peaks about 30 seconds after the cut
- What the gas does to the nerve endings in your cornea, and why your eyes water
- The missing piece: why garlic doesn't make you cry, and the second enzyme found in 2002
- Quick question: a sharp knife or a dull one?
- Why onions do this, and three ways to cry less: chill it, use a sharp knife, add a breeze

The drawings and the gas curve are simplified for teaching.
