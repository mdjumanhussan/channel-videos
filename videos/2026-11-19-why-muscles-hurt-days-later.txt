Title: Sore Two Days Later? It's Not Lactic Acid

Description:
The muscle ache that shows up a day or two after exercise is not caused by lactic acid, which is back to normal within about an hour. This short animated explainer shows what the timing tells us, why walking downhill or down stairs makes you sorest, and what the ache actually is.

What is covered:
- The delay: fine the next day, sore on day two
- Lactate (lactic acid): builds up while you push hard, back to normal within about an hour (1983 study)
- The timing: soreness usually starts 6 to 8 hours later and peaks about two days after
- The downhill clue: no rise in lactic acid, yet some of the worst soreness
- Quick question: up the stairs or down the stairs?
- Braking (eccentric) movements: muscles working while being pulled longer
- What hurts: tiny damage in the muscle, the repair, and more sensitive nerve endings
- Why the second time hurts less, and why soreness isn't a score

The legs, muscles and curves are simplified drawings; the soreness curve and bars are illustrative. This video explains how the body works; it is not medical advice.
