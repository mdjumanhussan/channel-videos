Title: Popcorn Is a Tiny Pressure Cooker

Description:
Every popcorn kernel is a tiny sealed pressure cooker, and the pop you hear is not the shell cracking. This short animated explainer follows one kernel from the pan to the jump on a millisecond clock, using a 2015 high-speed camera study.

What is covered:
- The three parts of a kernel: the hull, starch, and about 14% water
- Why steam builds up inside (about 10 bar, the researchers' estimate)
- The bursting point: about 180 °C (17 of 50 kernels popped at 170 °C, 48 of 50 at 180 °C)
- The pop on a clock: starch foam at 6.9 ms, a starch "leg" at 13.8 ms, the jump at 20.7 ms
- Quick question: what makes the pop sound?
- Why most cracks are silent, and the sound comes about 6 ms later as steam escapes
- Why some kernels never pop, and why ordinary corn doesn't pop like this

Kernels, gauges and sound waves are simplified drawings.
