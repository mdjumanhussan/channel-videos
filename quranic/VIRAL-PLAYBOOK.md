# Viral playbook — Quranic Story Reels

Research pass 2026-10-11. The daily job adds new findings at the bottom.
Accuracy and the visual rules in RUNBOOK.md always win over anything here.

## What Facebook rewards
- Watch time and **completion** (people watching to the end), then shares, comments and rewatches. A scroll-past in under 2 seconds ends a Reel's reach.
- New pages are tested on a small pool of non-followers first; strong retention pushes the Reel to bigger pools.
- Retention target: 60% average watch-through is the floor, 70%+ is excellent, 80%+ gets amplified.
- Original, clean, watermark-free uploads beat reposts.

## Length and pacing (biggest lever)
- Story Reels: **45–65 seconds**. Most top Reels are under 45 s; storytelling can stretch to about 60.
- Script **120–170 words**. One new image, verse or emotional beat every 3–5 seconds. No pause longer than half a second.
- If a story needs more, split it: "Part 1", "Part 2 tomorrow". Cliffhanger endings drive follows and return visits.

## The first 3 seconds
- Hook text is **on screen in frame 1**, before any animation. (Engine does this for title cards.)
- Hook = a question, a striking true moment or a promise of meaning. Examples: "He was thrown into a well by his own brothers.", "One sentence that made Allah forgive the first human.", "The sea split in two. Here's what happened next."
- Never clickbait: the hook must be true to the verses.

## Captions and text
- Over 80% of people start with the sound off: word-by-word captions on every spoken line.
- Keep text in the upper two-thirds and away from the right-side buttons. (Engine captions sit at y ≈ 1190, max width 820.)
- High contrast: white/gold with dark stroke.

## Sound
- Voice first. Clear on phone speakers: rumble cut, presence boost, gentle compression, −14 LUFS master. (Engine does this.)
- Deep impact "hit" on the title and on the emotional peak (`HITS` in the episode). Whooshes on transitions. Soft ambience under the voice. No music.
- Silence before the biggest line (a 0.4–0.6 s pause via `pause`) makes it land.

## Visuals and design
- One clear focal point per scene, strong colour on the thing that matters.
- Motion in every scene: camera drift plus particles, light, water or fire.
- Contrast between scenes (dark night → golden dawn) keeps eyes awake.
- Cover frame = the title card, so the feed thumbnail reads instantly.

## Caption text (post copy)
- Line 1 is the hook. Then 3–5 short lines with one verse quoted exactly and referenced. One-line lesson.
- Ask a soft question to invite comments ("Which part touched your heart?").
- 3–4 precise hashtags, not 30.
- First comment (`firstCommentText`): the full verse references and "Part 2 tomorrow, in sha Allah" when split.

## Posting
- One Reel a day, same time, so the audience learns when to expect it.
- After 30 days of data, move the time to Metricool's best time for the page.
- A breakout is about 10× the page's normal views. Study what that Reel did differently and log it.

## New findings (daily job appends here)
- 2026-10-11: Open one question in the first frame and do not answer it until late. Give enough context that a stranger knows the topic within a second, then hold the most interesting part back; closing the loop early makes people leave. For faith Reels: plain hook, focused middle, end on a strong line people are proud to share; stay sincere, no controversy. Applied in episode 2 (hook "Why did Allah send a crow?", answer in scene 6). The hook must still be true to the verses. Sources: https://postiz.com/blog/why-your-reels-arent-going-viral-hook-framework , https://onepathnetwork.com/?p=20642
- 2026-10-12: Emplifi's study of 10,110 Facebook Reels: human speech in the first 3 seconds raised 10-second retention by almost 25% compared with music, and vertical video got 20.9% more reach. We already speak from frame 1 and use no music; keep it. The finding on a person on screen in the first seconds does not apply (no figures, by rule). Applied today as a cold open: drop the intro line and start inside the most dramatic moment, so the voice says the hook over the strongest picture. Source: https://socialmediatoday.com/news/human-speech-and-presence-help-drive-reels-engagement/815381
- 2026-10-13: "Wrong answer" pivot hook: set up the outcome the viewer (or the people in the story) expect, then say it was wrong, and hold the real answer back. Pair the spoken hook with the same words on screen so it works with sound off, and front-load the key word in the first second. Applied in episode 4: "They saw a cloud coming and said: rain! They were wrong." (true to 46:24), with the answer in scene 7. Source: https://www.opus.pro/blog/facebook-reels-hook-formulas
- 2026-10-14: Facebook decides in the first 2–3 seconds whether to keep showing a Reel, and openings with "a logo, a slow zoom, a title card" lose people before the message starts. Put the strongest line first and keep the lead-in under 2 seconds before speech. If everyone leaves at 2 s, the hook is the problem; if they leave at 80%, the ending is too long. No links in on-screen text. Applied in episode 5: no title-card look on frame 1 (plain headline over the story picture, no ornament or kicker). Source: https://www.lilachbullock.com/why-your-facebook-reels-get-no-views/
- 2026-10-15: Pacing guidance for Reels: script at about 2.5–3 words per second, a new image or beat every 3–5 seconds, no silence over 0.5 s, and for longer story Reels a fresh hook roughly every 10 seconds; if average watch is under 60% of length, tighten the pace or shorten. Our Reels have run at about 2.3 words/s. Applied in episode 6: voice speed 1.1 (170 words in 60 s, about 2.8 words/s), first picture change at about 4 s, and a mid-story re-hook (the people come back and ask who did it). Source: https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-facebook-reels-length-format-retention
- 2026-10-16: Open on action, not setup: the first frame should already be mid-motion (the example contrasts hands at work with a slow shopfront zoom), and the hook line should take under 2 seconds to say. If viewers drop before 3 s, recut the hook. Our openings have all been still paintings with a slow zoom. Applied in episode 7: a live flickering fire from frame 1 (new engine fx `flames`) and a 6-word first sentence. Source: https://www.lilachbullock.com/what-makes-a-facebook-reel-go-hot-and-get-more-views/
- 2026-10-17: Read the retention graph, not the view count: if most viewers leave around 2 s, the hook is the problem; if they stay to about 80% then leave, the ending runs too long. One creator moved the most useful moment from 0:14 to the opening and saw a 3–6x lift on the same content. Our own data agrees: Reel 6 (idols), with its mid-story re-hook and cliffhanger, grew to about 5x our usual views two days after posting. Applied in episode 8: the son's question is in the first line, and a second open loop starts the moment the first is answered. Source: https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=94481
- 2026-10-18: End with the takeaway as big on-screen text, not only in the spoken line: a brief text recap of the main point in the last seconds helps sound-off viewers and invites rewatches; a strong loop is a final line that sets up the opening question; label multi-part content ("Part 2") to keep people for the sequence. Our own data: Reels 6 and 7 (a linked two-part Ibrahim arc, 6 ending on a cliffhanger into 7) both broke out on their second day (1253 and 1376 views, 10–11 s average). Applied in episode 9: a "The lesson" card with the one-line takeaway on screen while it is spoken (new engine option `lesson`). Source: https://socialk.it/en/blog/instagram-reels-retention-strategy
- 2026-10-19: Facebook tests a Reel on a small audience first and widens it in stages only if completion holds, so reach often arrives late; posting time mainly matters for the first hour's test sample. Our data matches: Reels 6, 7 and 8 were ordinary at 15 h and broke out on day 2 (Reel 8: 243 views at 15 h, 4732 by day 2). So judge a Reel on day 2, not day 1, and keep openings tight for that first test group. No build today (Reel 9 is being re-posted by hand), so not applied yet. Source: https://www.lilachbullock.com/what-makes-a-facebook-reel-go-hot-and-get-more-views/
- 2026-10-20: Keep text inside the Reels safe zone. Meta's Reels guidance says to keep key information within safe zones so the app's interface does not cover it; for a 1080x1920 Reel the published margins are about 14% at the top (269 px), 35% at the bottom (672 px) and 6% on each side (65 px), where the caption text, page name and buttons sit. Our karaoke captions were centred at y 1190, so 2-line captions reached about y 1280, under the overlay. Applied in episode 10: captions moved up (new engine option `CAPTION_Y`, set to 1110) so they end above y ~1240.
